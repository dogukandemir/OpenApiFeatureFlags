# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. This project is maintained by one person in their own time, and contributions
come from strangers in every timezone, so the bar is simply: treat others as you would want to be
treated if you had spent your Saturday on this.

## Expected behaviour

- Assume competence and good faith. Ask what someone was trying to achieve before assuming they were
  careless.
- Critique the code, the design and the evidence. Not the person, their experience, or their English.
  Many contributors are writing in a second language; that is not a defect to be corrected.
- Be specific. "This breaks X because Y" is useful. "This is bad" is not.
- Accept a reasoned "no". A maintainer declining a change is not a verdict on you, and a stale issue
  is not a personal slight.
- Respect the licence, and respect that the project's stated scope is the project's to decide.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, threats, or sustained disruption of discussion.
- Discriminatory or demeaning comments about any person or group, including on the basis of race,
  ethnicity, nationality, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, age, religion, or
  level of experience.
- Sexual attention or imagery in any project space.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Deliberately derailing issues or pull requests, or reopening a settled decision to relitigate it.

## Where this applies

Every project space: issues, pull requests, commit messages, code comments, and any place somebody is
representing the project. It also applies to behaviour that makes people afraid to participate.

## Reporting and enforcement

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, contact the maintainer
[@dogukandemir](https://github.com/dogukandemir) privately — for example by using GitHub's private
reporting or a direct message. Please do not open a public issue about it.

Reports are read by the maintainer alone and kept confidential. What happens next depends on the
severity and the pattern: a private word, a request to edit or delete a comment, a temporary block
from the repository, or a permanent ban. The maintainer will say what they are doing and why, unless
doing so would expose the reporter.

If the report concerns the maintainer, there is no neutral party here — that limitation is worth
stating plainly rather than pretending otherwise. In that case, use GitHub's
[report abuse](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) channel.

## Attribution

This is a short, purpose-written policy rather than an adaptation of a longer template. If you want a
fuller, widely adopted document, the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/) is
the usual choice and is freely reusable.
